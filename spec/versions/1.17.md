# Version 1.17

Version 1.17 states, in the design and in the contracts that own each part, how Hive keeps the memory its sessions load, so that it never needs a cleanup pass.

At its start the Hive or a Fleet loads only the `always` tier of its role, the rules every session of that role keeps from its first turn, under a small budget. Every other operating rule is filed under its subject and reaches a supervisor in the response of the command that performs a step its subject's `loads_at` names, `dispatch`, `gate`, `define`, `stow` or `project`, recorded as received for the session's generation and never left to a session's judgment of a description. A Swarm or a Lictor is given its Imperative and nothing else from the home: `hive-imperative render` writes into its Rules, after its class's playbook, the projections of the subjects its Hunt's definition names in the specification its Imperative binds, and its delivery records every rule and revision written into it for its generation, as a supervisor's launch records the projections it loads; an Imperative too large for its transport is refused and its Hunt narrowed, never trimmed. A ruling is an operating rule, which renders one line, or a product ruling, which is recorded into the specification of the work it governs and renders none; the recorder states which, and a ruling that is both is two records. No free-text memory exists to be loaded: a stow writes a rule record, a fact of its subject, a line on the task record or a note in the maintenance queue, and a session loads only rendered projections. The design states this in [Definitions, standing rules and generated knowledge](../../docs/design.md#definitions-standing-rules-and-generated-knowledge), and its components, the Imperative, the checkpoint, the restart, the stow, a worker's retirement, the UI's rows, the record model, the plan and the evidence still required point to it.

The fold drains the whole maintenance queue, each note into the facts of its subject under that subject's budget, inside every stow and before every checkpoint. It is never optional, its receipt is part of the stow's, and a stow whose queue is not drained is not reset-safe, so no checkpoint passes and no restart follows. A worker's notes enter the queue at its retirement or its collection; a note with no subject, or one over its subject's budget, becomes a Pool item for the supervisor, and none parks in the `always` tier. Each projection has its own budget in one configuration record of the Pool, the budget record, changed only on the Hive Mind's word: the Hive's `always` projection 18 KB, a Fleet's own `always` projection 4.5 KB and each subject's projection 9 KB, a KB being 1,024 bytes. A rule line is at most 300 characters, and a write to the rule store whose line is longer, or after which a projection would exceed its budget, is refused at the write with `pool.rule_line_too_long` or `pool.projection_over_budget`, naming the recorder's three moves: shorten the line and file its mechanics as a fact of its subject, file the rule into a new, narrower subject, or supersede a rule. Only an `always` rule that cannot fit, or a subject that cannot be split, reaches the Hive Mind, as one decision naming the rule and the two ways out. A second rule on a subject in force at one scope is refused at the write with `pool.rule_conflict` unless it names the first, and a rule line is reworded by a superseding rule whose words are unchanged. The budgets are guarded by counting misses: a refusal, a hold or a Synapse finding that cites a rule for which the acting generation holds no receipt, whether from its launch, a command's delivery or the delivery of its Imperative, which lists every rule and revision it carried, records a miss against that rule's subject and the step, source health shows each subject's misses beside its size, and a subject's second miss raises one decision proposing its re-tiering or a new step in its `loads_at`.

[pool.md](../pool.md#rules) states these in its Rules, The Imperative, Generated knowledge, Reading the Pool, error codes and proofs; [test-targets.md](../test-targets.md#the-targets) adds the proofs of `test-pool`, `test-dispatch` and `test-ui`; and [decisions.md](../decisions.md#left-to-the-stage-that-needs-it) leaves to the stage that builds each step's command the member of its response that carries what it delivers, with the call from `synapse` to `pool` that the gate round's delivery needs.

No schema changes. The version adds record kinds described in prose: the budget record, a subject's facts, the maintenance queue and its notes, and the record of a miss, with the operation `fold` of `hive-memory` and the receipts the declaration will register for them. Each enters its schema with the change to [pool.md](../pool.md#left-undecided) that adds the members of a rule's kind and tier before `hive-rule` is built, which also settles how a rule's subject key relates to the subject record it is filed under; the member of a worker's report that carries its notes enters with stage 4. It adds two error codes, `pool.rule_line_too_long` and `pool.projection_over_budget`, and makes `pool.rule_conflict` a refusal of `record` as well. It is a minor change by the rule of [spec/README.md](../README.md#changing-a-contract) and the precedent of 1.7 and 1.10 to 1.16: it changes the prose of the design and the contracts and adds new kinds, an operation and error codes, which an existing reader can ignore, and it adds no schema, no member, no package and no dependency and changes no package declaration. Every record and declaration valid under 1.16 is valid under 1.17, and it asks nothing of code already built, so the library's contract version constant does not move.

# Dispatch and supervision contract

Owner: `dispatch`, with observation by `shadow` and durable work identity in `pool`. This file carries the design's prospective stage 4 behaviour and acceptance cases. Its record and operation schemas enter with that stage; these requirements add no new authority or executor.

## Dispatch matrix

The matrix is one plain-JSON record of the home, written only through a policy request with Accepted and Applied receipts. The resolver reads it at dispatch and at each fixer's launch; workers receive a resolved profile and never read the matrix. Its entries hold:

- Hive and Fleet supervision profiles, distinct from worker classes.
- Classes 0–7, each with eligible members per harness, placement preferences naming supported backends or policy, and an optional Lictor member override.
- A default profile and selection data: provider weights, gauge comparisons, conditional member substitutions, floors with comparison operators, and tie rules. Each selection rule carries scope, provenance and expiry. Temporary vendor preferences remain temporary rule records.
- A gate entry with configured reviewer roles resolved for class 2, validation steps, a fixer, its explicit step-up member and `after_round`, fix-turn budgets, check bounds and escalation signals. Roles add no dispatch class. The class-1 step-up stays in the fixer's harness and vendor.
- Per profile: harness, real model name accepted by its CLI, effort, capabilities and selection rationale with source and date. A harness-to-vendor table supports vendor-relative comparisons.

The matrix supports any number of vendors. A class's first and second member may name the same vendor. The gate's reviewer roles are whatever its effective entry names, with the same vendor allowed; no counterpart or reviewer-diversity rule constrains that choice. Route qualification checks the reviewer results the effective configured gate requires against what the installed plugin actually supplies, never an independent fixed-count assumption. Adapter facts and operator scope rulings remain named; policy chooses roles and classes through the matrix.

`test-dispatch` proves supervision resolves the respective entries, a changed member is used at the next launch without changing a running generation, the Lictor override affects the intended role only, floor equality and ties follow data, a cross-vendor step-up is refused, and same-vendor first and second members and reviewer roles are accepted. It also proves a matrix with more than two vendors resolves by the same rules. Route qualification probes each real model name and effort before launch. `test-synapse` proves missing or substituted reviewer results cannot satisfy the effective gate entry.

## Launch qualification and recovery

**Recovery at a shared boundary.** Per-task exclusion does not serialize a shared repository or registry mutation. Fleet recovery uses bounded serialization or retry at that shared mutation boundary, retains each task’s identity and records contention separately from a failed launch; exhausted contention leaves recovery pending with its cause. Acceptance: recover two tasks concurrently against one shared mutation boundary; both complete without duplicate launches, or the contending task remains pending with a bounded-retry receipt, never a launch-failure receipt. Sources: [upstream firstmate/pull/7154](https://github.com/kunchenguid/firstmate/pull/7154).

**Permission waits after launch.** Pre-answering startup prompts does not answer later permission or trust requests. The adapter observes those waits throughout the session and promptly routes the request and its authority boundary to the owning supervisor through the Shadow; only a request within delegated authority may be answered there, and an operator-only request reaches the Hive Mind through that supervisor. Acceptance: a session that passed startup later asks for permission and trust; each becomes a correlated actionable wait within the local observation bound, with its required authority, and neither quiet activity nor startup approval answers it. Sources: [upstream firstmate/issues/7105](https://github.com/kunchenguid/firstmate/issues/7105).

**Effective launch restrictions.** Every launch qualifies the effective tool and plug-in restrictions needed for its reporting route and Imperative against supported harness capabilities. Prompt instructions alone do not prove that the route is enforced. A capability gap holds launch; qualification writes no global configuration and creates no credential-bearing environment file. Harness-owned plug-in configuration remains outside Hive’s records. Acceptance: an installed plug-in that would bypass the supervisor route is disabled or restricted for the launch by verified supported capabilities, or the launch is held; a fake unsupported CLI proves the hold, unchanged global settings and absence of a credential-bearing environment file. Sources: [upstream firstmate/issues/7221](https://github.com/kunchenguid/firstmate/issues/7221).

**A wake needs an outcome.** A readiness wake is acknowledged only with a recorded launch, a verified live reason for withholding that launch, or a handoff to the owning supervisor. A typed ready state, a handled message or a statement that dispatch is next is not that outcome; an unresolved wake remains durable for reconciliation. Acceptance: a ready Pool item whose launcher was never called stays actionable after restart; a recorded launch, current capacity or authority withholding, and an ownership handoff each acknowledge only their own wake and generation. Sources: [upstream firstmate/issues/7204](https://github.com/kunchenguid/firstmate/issues/7204).

## Optional browser proof

**Optional browser proof qualification.** The selected browser-task route proves desktop automation identity isolation as well as profile isolation, so automation cannot take over the operator’s desktop browser or its external-link and sign-in routing. It never attaches to a live operator session. Each session records its lifecycle owner; a caller-owned browser retains that owner and is not closed or replaced by Hive. An owned session has bounded idle and orphan retirement through the process-execution contract, rather than depending on a worker remembering to stop it. Acceptance: run isolated automation while the operator’s browser is open and follow an external link and a sign-in redirect: both retain their intended desktop identity; caller-owned browser state survives task completion, while an owned browser left by a crashed worker retires within its bound. Sources: [upstream chrome-devtools-axi/pull/167](https://github.com/kunchenguid/chrome-devtools-axi/pull/167), [upstream chrome-devtools-axi/issues/166](https://github.com/kunchenguid/chrome-devtools-axi/issues/166), [upstream chrome-devtools-axi/issues/169](https://github.com/kunchenguid/chrome-devtools-axi/issues/169).

**Measured page-bound capture.** An action and its read-back share one task, selected-page identity and page generation. Capture records the actual viewport measured after the operation, not the requested setting; a selection or generation change invalidates the pair and leaves proof pending. Acceptance: a requested viewport differs from the resulting page size and the receipt records the measured dimensions; a second caller changes the selected page between action and capture and the stale pair is rejected rather than attached to the Hunt. Sources: [upstream chrome-devtools-axi/pull/172](https://github.com/kunchenguid/chrome-devtools-axi/pull/172), [upstream chrome-devtools-axi/issues/154](https://github.com/kunchenguid/chrome-devtools-axi/issues/154).
